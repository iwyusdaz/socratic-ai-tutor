# Consent text (prepared — not yet run)

Read aloud before the session; give the participant a copy. Collect only a yes/no plus a participant code — no signature scan, no personal data beyond what the participant volunteers in conversation.

---

## Script

"Thank you for helping. You're taking part in an informal usability session for a school project: an AI math tutor that is being designed and evaluated.

What will happen: you'll spend up to an hour solving a few algebra problems. For some you'll work with an AI tutor I've built, thinking out loud as you go. For others you'll work on paper without any AI. I'll ask a few questions at the end. The whole thing takes 45–60 minutes.

What I record: notes about how the session went, and — only if you agree — a screen and audio recording. I'll refer to you by a code (like P3), never your name. I won't collect your contact details, and I'll remove anything identifying you from quotes.

What it's for: to find problems with the tutor's design so it can be improved. You are not being tested — there is no pass or fail, and getting stuck is genuinely useful information.

Your choices: you can skip any question, take a break, or stop the session at any time, for any reason, without giving a reason. You can ask me to delete your notes or recording within 14 days of the session.

Risks and benefits: the main inconvenience is your time. There's no compensation, and no benefit to you beyond the session itself. The tutor may make math mistakes — don't take its answers as ground truth.

Do you agree to take part? [Record yes/no]
Do you agree to screen and audio recording? [Record yes/no — notes-only if no]"

## Data handling (for the organizer)

- Store notes and recordings locally, encrypted or in an access-controlled folder; never in a public repository.
- Publish only anonymized quotes tied to participant codes.
- Delete recordings after analysis (suggested: within 30 days) or on request.
- The session is with adults only. If a potential participant is under 18, do not proceed — that would require school and parent approval and a privacy review.
- If a participant shares personal information during the session, do not record it in notes; ask them not to share it and continue.
